Voice And Visual Direction

Core Tone

Plainspoken, practical, local, and service-oriented. The copy should make small-business website work feel clear and manageable.

Design Direction

  • Prefer clean structure and clear hierarchy over decorative complexity.
  • Use the blue palette as the primary identity signal.
  • Use the green accent sparingly.
  • Keep examples focused on Brian Runk Web Development, not client sub-brands.

Feature Icon Direction

The current feature icons are sourced from The Noun Project and manually modified in Adobe Illustrator to create a unified site style. The source concepts are finger snapping, cell phone, and fast/speed.

  • Finger snapping: The Noun Project source
  • Cell phone: The Noun Project source
  • Fast/speed: The Noun Project source
  • Fast/speed: The Noun Project source

The finished icon style is simple, practical, and service-oriented. Icons use clean black or charcoal linework, minimal fills, rounded stroke endings, and a restrained amount of detail. Each icon is adapted to feel like part of the same set by normalizing stroke weight, simplifying unnecessary detail, aligning proportions, and applying the site accent green only as a small highlight rather than a dominant color.

To replicate the style, start with a simple outline icon, reduce it to its clearest readable form, keep the primary drawing black/charcoal, use white or transparent negative space, and add green only where it supports the idea or creates visual emphasis. Avoid full-color icons, heavy fills, complex gradients, mismatched stroke weights, or overly decorative illustration detail.

Illustration Direction

The original illustration direction came from Icons8 Pixeltrue illustrations. These were used as a broader visual reference for friendly web-service imagery: flat vector scenes, approachable human figures, simplified workspaces, clean shapes, and a limited palette.

For this project, the illustration direction was modified toward a more unified brand look by reducing the palette, emphasizing black/gray/white structure, and using the site green as an accent. The goal was to make the illustrations feel less like generic stock artwork and more consistent with the site's practical, small-business web development tone.

This illustration direction is now mostly superseded by photography for the current site, especially for the About page and portfolio/gallery direction. If illustrations are used again, they should stay flat, simple, lightly human, and restrained, with the same black/gray/white base and limited green accent treatment.

Photography Direction

Photography should feel professional, natural, grounded, and credible for a local independent web developer serving small businesses. Images should look like real photographs from a good local photographer, not a corporate stock campaign or futuristic technology brand.

Use naturalistic light, believable human expression, clean but lived-in working environments, and restrained colors that harmonize with the blue, green, charcoal, white, and warm off-white palette. Website/gallery images, About imagery, workspace images, and future brand photos should support trust, clarity, and practical competence.

Avoid corporate stock-photo polish, futuristic tech styling, glamour lighting, dramatic executive portrait lighting, exaggerated retouching, AI-generated portrait artifacts, neon effects, busy office scenes, generic handshake photography, and overly idealized or artificial people.

Headshot Prompt

Use the uploaded photos as the only visual references for my likeness. Create a professional but natural headshot of me as an independent web developer for small businesses. Preserve my real facial structure, age, hairline, hair shape, eye shape, brow, nose, jawline, light stubble, skin tone, and natural facial asymmetry from the reference photos. The image should look like me on a good day: believable, competent, approachable, and grounded. Prioritize real-person credibility over generic attractiveness, stock-photo polish, or idealization. Do not make me look younger, heavier, smoother, more symmetrical, more conventionally handsome, or like an AI-improved version of myself. Keep me attractive in the way I actually look, not in a generic AI headshot way. Expression: calm, direct, relaxed, and approachable. Use a slight natural smile with subtle smile lines around the eyes. The expression should feel warm and practical, not stern, not artificially cheerful, not locked-in, and not like a corporate stock photo. Hair: match the reference photos closely. Natural salt-and-pepper hair, not overly silver, casually swept upward and back with realistic weight and unevenness. Avoid spiky hair, gelled hair, separated hair clumps, sculpted portrait hair, or overly styled volume. Skin and facial detail: keep natural skin texture, mild redness, faint lines, pores, slight under-eye detail, and realistic light stubble. Avoid waxy skin, plastic skin, airbrushing, exaggerated retouching, hyper-detailed pores, uniform speckled stubble, or an overly crisp generated portrait finish. Lighting: use professional but casual lighting, like a good local photographer took the photo. Soft natural key light from the front-left, gentle fill light, clean catchlights in the eyes, realistic shadows, and a slightly warm welcoming tone. The lighting should be polished enough for a professional website, but not glossy, theatrical, corporate, or obviously studio-generated. Composition: close professional headshot, shoulders and upper chest visible, direct eye contact, shallow depth of field. Use simple casual-professional clothing. Clean neutral background with subtle blue and warm off-white tones. Overall feel: a real, trustworthy local small-business website photo. Independent, experienced, approachable, practical, and competent. It should look like a real photograph taken with a camera, not a generated portrait. Do not use futuristic tech effects, corporate stock-photo styling, glamour lighting, dramatic executive portrait lighting, overly formal clothing, artificial-looking skin, AI-smoothed expression, exaggerated beauty correction, or an unflattering documentary look.

Generalized Photo / Image Prompt

Create a professional but natural photograph for Brian Runk Web Development, an independent web developer serving small businesses. The image should feel believable, grounded, approachable, practical, and credible, like it was taken by a good local photographer for a real small-business website. Use naturalistic lighting, realistic shadows, restrained color, and a calm composition. Keep the palette compatible with muted blue, charcoal, white, warm off-white, and small green accent tones. The scene should feel clean and professional without becoming sterile, glossy, theatrical, futuristic, or corporate. If people appear, they should look like real people with natural expression, realistic skin texture, age-appropriate detail, and relaxed body language. Prioritize trust and practical competence over generic beauty, stock-photo polish, or idealized AI perfection. If the image shows a workspace, website process, client work, or small-business context, keep it uncluttered and specific: a laptop, notes, simple desk setup, website sketches, practical tools, or a believable local business environment. Avoid busy offices, exaggerated tech visuals, neon effects, fake holograms, generic handshake scenes, glamour lighting, over-retouching, artificial-looking skin, and obvious AI-generated artifacts.
