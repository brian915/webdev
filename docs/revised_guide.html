Brian Runk Web Development

Visual and content style guide

Brand Basics

Clear

The site should quickly explain what the business does, who it helps, and how to get in touch.

Modern

The design should feel current, practical, and trustworthy without becoming trendy or overdesigned.

Easy To Use

Pages should be readable, mobile-friendly, and direct. The next step should be easy to find.

Color Roles & Palette

The palette should feel warm, practical, trustworthy, and restrained.

Contrast Combinations & Legibility

STANDARD DOCUMENT SURFACE

Background & Text Primary

A clean white surface with dark foreground text, creating the clearest and most neutral reading experience.
Use for main page content, body copy, headings, and normal document-style sections.

SUBTLE BLOCK

Background Subtle & Text Primary

A warm off-white surface with dark foreground text, creating quiet contrast without distracting attention
Use for soft content bands, panel backgrounds, call boxes, secondary content bands, and readable supporting sections.

ALT SUBTLE BLOCk

Background Subtle Alt & Text Primary

A soft lavender-gray surface with dark foreground text, creating a more distinct yet restrained secondary surface.
Use for secondary cards, sidebar widgets, table headers, content containers, subtle gradients and promotional surfaces.

PRIMARY ACTION/BRAND EMPHASIS

Accent & Background

A blue brand-accent surface with light text, creating clear emphasis and strong action-oriented contrast.
Use for primary buttons, key links, header stripes, primary brand accents and emphasis.

INVERSE/HIGH CONTRAST

Accent Dark & Background

A dark blue accent surface with light text, the strongest high-contrast treatment in the core palette.
Use for headers, footers, callout panels, support treatments, hover states, interactive elements and high-impact sections

BRAND/THEMATIC CALLOUT

Accent Secondary & Background

A purple accent surface with light text, creating a bold non-blue emphasis
Use for promotional calloutss, thematic sections, review elements and other highlights, and distinct brand moments.

STATUS CONFIRMATION

Confirmation & Background

A deep green surface with light text, creating a positive signal.
Use for success messages, save confirmations, completed states, and affirmative UI feedback.

STATUS ALERT

Alert & Background

A muted red surface with light text, creating a clear warning signal without becoming visually harsh.
Use for warnings, errors, destructive action labels, and urgent notes.

SECONDARY INFORMATION & MARKERS

Border & Text Primary

A light neutral surface with dark text, low-emphasis contrast for information that should remain readable but secondary.
Use for dividing lines, metadata, helper text areas, captions, disabled states, and lower-priority information.

Photography & Image Direction

Photography Direction

Photography should feel grounded, practical, and real. Avoid generic corporate polish or stock-photo artificiality.

Image Direction

Images should support trust, clarity, and small-business credibility.

Headshot Prompt

Create a professional but natural headshot for an independent web developer serving small businesses. The image should feel believable, competent, approachable, and grounded. Preserve real facial structure, age, hairline, hair shape, skin texture, expression, and natural asymmetry.

Generalized Photo / Image Prompt

Create a clean, practical, small-business website image with warm neutral lighting, clear composition, and a grounded local-business feel. Avoid futuristic effects, excessive gloss, generic office stock imagery, and decorative clutter.

Voice & Copy Direction

Core Tone

Plainspoken, useful, grounded, and direct.

Written Voice / Copy Tone

  • Use concrete language.
  • Avoid hype and exaggerated claims.
  • Explain practical benefits clearly.
  • Keep sentences readable and direct.
  • Make the next action easy to understand.

Preferred Copy Shape

Lead with the customer’s need, explain the practical problem, show how the website helps, and make the next step obvious.
